# ChemMon Change Management

<!-- Source: EFSA Supporting Publications - 2026 -  - Chemical monitoring reporting guidance  2026 data collection.pdf pp. 10-16 -->

## Rule Summary

- Ordinary annual changes proposed in October-November of year X usually apply to results reported in year X+2.
- The X+2 delay exists because sampling officers need requirements before samples are collected.
- Urgent legal changes can apply sooner when required by new or amended legislation.
- The 2026 change table classifies changes by legal requirement, quality requirement, or sampling requirement.

## Revision Cadence

For national annual monitoring, changes proposed to elements, catalogues, or business rules in October-November of year X can generally only be applied to results reported in year X+2. (ChemMon 2026 p10)

This delay exists because changes need to be known before sampling officers collect samples in year X+1. A new facet or element requirement cannot fairly be applied to samples that were already taken before the rule existed. (ChemMon 2026 p10)

The exception is a change required by new or amended legislation that could not be anticipated by EFSA. Those changes are applied as soon as legally required. (ChemMon 2026 p10)

Network members are expected to contribute to the revision process continuously, with emphasis on suggestions submitted during the last quarter of each year, especially September-November. (ChemMon 2026 p10)

## Main Change Categories for 2026

The 2026 guidance lists major changes in Table 1. They fall into three broad categories:

- **Legal requirement**: new or amended regulations, such as new `progLegalRef` N422A for Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2019/1873 on intensified official controls at border control posts.
- **Quality requirement**: tightened or clarified business rules to improve data quality.
- **Sampling requirement**: additions to permitted combinations of programme type, legal reference, and sampling strategy, such as ST20A with K009A/K018A for pesticides.

(ChemMon 2026 pp. 11-16)
